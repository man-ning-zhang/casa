<h3 class="entry-card__title">《枯》 (<em>Withered</em>)</h3>
<p class="entry-card__meta">A visual micro-fiction in fifty letters &middot; EN/CN</p>
<div class="entry-card__body">
<p>Henry Frost arrived in Boston in 1852 and spent his life recording bodies, first the names in a YMCA gym register, then his own. Ms. Yan, a fitness coach in Beijing in 2026, logs her steps, sleep, and heart rate on a watch. They write to each other across 174 years and never meet. Neither one began: each kept recording on their own side until the two frequencies matched and the letters appeared in between. Cause and effect form a loop here, not a line.</p>
<p>The homepage is a chain of musical notes running from 1852 to 2026, with the diary and the letters sharing one staff. The connection ends Frost's long solitude, but it also makes him mortal: to be linked is to begin to age, and finally to end. A person, the letters suggest, is not kept alive by living but by someone recording them, day after day, without missing one.</p>

### How to experience it

- **Turn the sound on.** Browsers need a gesture first, so click the first note, or the music button at the lower right, to start the score.
- Follow the chain of notes to open the fifty letters. Drag the two half moons at the top together to make a full moon.
- On a desktop, hold Ctrl and scroll, or pinch on a trackpad: the notes dissolve into 24 archival images from the Boston YMCA, 1851 to 2018.
- Clicking the last note opens the final letter and burns it a few seconds later, and all the notes fall. That is the ending, not a bug.
- A small surprise: at the very bottom of the page, click my name five times in quick succession.

It works on both phones and desktops. On a phone, tap the notes and drag the moons; the archival reveal is most reliable on a desktop, where the zoom gesture is explicit.

### A note on the text

Built under hackathon time pressure, the fifty letters were drafted from archival material and the podcast corpus, and they still carry the seams of that process. When there is more time, I plan to revise them by hand, one letter at a time.
