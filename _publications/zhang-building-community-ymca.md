This paper draws on interviews with 23 YMCA members from diverse immigration, racial/ethnic, gender, and socioeconomic backgrounds, as well as ethnographic observations at 13 YMCA branches in Greater Boston. I also draw on historical materials from YMCA archives to examine how fitness practices contribute to community-making. By connecting contemporary experiences of exercise with the YMCA's longstanding institutional mission, the study shows how physical activity can foster social ties, cultivate belonging, and sustain civic engagement amid broader processes of social fragmentation.
