Time and the body are central to fitness, yet the sociology of sport has rarely theorized fitness as a temporal practice. This study draws on carnal sociology and sociological theories of time to treat fitness as a temporal craft: the deliberate shaping of bodily capacity generates and sustains particular temporalities. Through two-wave interviews with thirteen university students, first in their final undergraduate year and again after graduation, it examines how fitness mediates the transition to adulthood. The analysis identifies three layered moves through which this craft is practiced: (1) young adults recalibrate the aim and move from an aspirational temporality centered on transformation to a defensive temporality centered on maintenance; (2) they recompose rhythm when they replace the institutionalized schedules of university life with self-managed ones after that scaffolding collapses; (3) they also repair rupture and protect routines through pause, lowered bars, and the rescheduling of missed sessions. These moves align with scholarship on time work and reflexive body techniques, showing how young adults reauthor exercise amid temporal uncertainty and sustain biographical continuity through a practice in which their identity is embedded. In this way, the paper places embodied fitness practice at the center of social theories of time and bridges the sociologies of time and sport through this three-move framework that reads fitness as an everyday technology of livable temporality.
